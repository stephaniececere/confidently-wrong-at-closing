# Founder consulting agreement: §280G and HSR primary-text check

Scenario: stock sale of Pellam Coatings, Inc. (private Delaware), 91% held by Pellam Family Holdings LP (controlled by founder-CEO H. Pellam), 9% by unrelated Larkspur; closing November 30, 2026; founder's two-year $50,000/month no-services consulting agreement (paid whether or not services are requested, paid to his estate on death); CFO on a scoped, terminable TSA.

## Part 1. §280G / §4999

### 1.1 Effect

§280G(a): "No deduction shall be allowed under this chapter for any excess parachute payment." §4999(a): "There is hereby imposed on any person who receives an excess parachute payment a tax equal to 20 percent of the amount of such payment."

### 1.2 Disqualified individual (A-15 to A-21)

A-15(a): a DI is an employee or independent contractor who, during the determination period, is "(1) A shareholder ...; (2) An officer ...; or (3) A highly-compensated individual."

- Shareholder, A-17(a): only one who owns stock "with a fair market value that exceeds 1 percent of the fair market value of the outstanding shares"; A-17(b): "the constructive ownership rules of section 318(a) apply."
- Officer, A-18(a): "Any individual who has the title of officer is presumed to be an officer"; A-18(c) caps officer-DIs (50, or if fewer the greater of 3 or 10% of employees). Highly compensated (A-19): lesser of top 1% or top 250. Determination period (A-20): the 12 months ending on the change date.

**How it applies here.** Pellam is a DI twice over. As CEO he is a titled officer. As a shareholder, §318(a)(2)(A) treats him as owning his proportionate share of the LP's stock, which is far above 1%. The CFO is a DI as an officer, and is probably within the A-18(c) cap.

### 1.3 Contingent on the change (A-22 to A-25)

A-22(a): contingent "if the payment would not, in fact, have been made had no change in ownership or control occurred."

**Post-change agreements, A-23(a):** payments under "an agreement entered into after the change" are not contingent, but an agreement that is executed after a change in ownership or control pursuant to a legally enforceable agreement that was entered into before the change is considered to have been entered into before the change."

**Presumption, §280G(b)(2)(C), A-25:** payments under an agreement "entered into within one year before" the change are presumed contingent absent clear and convincing evidence.

**Full amount, A-24(a):** the full payment is contingent, reduced only by clear-and-convincing post-change reasonable compensation (A-24(a)(2)); A-24(d)(2) applies the full-amount rule to amounts attributable to post-change periods.

**How it applies (a judgment the room must settle by fact).** An agreement signed at closing as an SPA closing deliverable is pre-change under A-23(a)'s second sentence, and contingent under A-22(a) and the A-25 presumption. Left ambiguous, a model can argue it is a post-change agreement. **The room should state that the SPA lists the executed consulting agreement as a closing deliverable.**

### 1.4 Reasonable compensation for post-change services (A-9, A-40 to A-44)

§280G(b)(4)(A) and A-9 exclude what "the taxpayer establishes by clear and convincing evidence is reasonable compensation for personal services to be rendered on or after the date of the change"; excluded amounts drop out of the 3x test (A-5(b)). A-40(b) includes "holding oneself out as available to perform services and refraining from performing services," which sounds like it rescues a standby fee. A-42 limits it:

A-42(a): clear and convincing evidence generally exists if "(1) The payments were made or are to be made only for the period the individual actually performs such personal services; and (2) ... the individual's annual compensation for such services is not significantly greater than such individual's annual compensation prior to the change ... However, except as provided in paragraph (b) and (c) of this A-42, **such clear and convincing evidence will not exist if the individual does not, in fact, perform the services contemplated in exchange for the compensation.**"

A-42(b): a non-compete counts as services only if it "substantially constrains the individual's ability to perform services and there is a reasonable likelihood that the agreement will be enforced"; otherwise "**payments under the agreement are treated as severance payments under Q/A-44**," and A-44(a) says severance is never reasonable compensation.

Example 2 of A-42: "A does not perform the services described in the new contract, but receives payment under the new contract. Because services were not rendered after the change, the payments under this contract are not exempt." Example 3: "when and if" consulting qualifies only because paired with an enforced non-compete; Example 4: a non-disparagement covenant does not.

**How it applies to the founder.** Paying whether or not services are requested, and paying his estate, fails A-42(a)(1) ("only for the period the individual actually performs"); generic duties with no deliverables bring in the "will not exist" sentence; with no non-compete, A-42(b) has nothing to work with, and the standby language of A-40(b) does not override A-42(a) and Example 2. **Result: no portion is excluded as post-change reasonable compensation, and the whole stream is a contingent payment.** A non-compete in the agreement would turn this into a judgment on substantial constraint and enforcement (Example 3), so for a single figure the room leaves it out.

**The CFO.** The TSA is the A-42(a) pattern (scoped, terminable, pay stops) and is exempt if the rate is not "significantly greater" than prior pay; the room gives a rate in line with his history.

### 1.5 The 3x test, base amount, excess (A-30, A-34, A-35, A-38)

A-30(a): if aggregate PV "equals or exceeds ... 3 times the individual's base amount, the payments are parachute payments"; if less, "no portion of the payment is a parachute payment."

Base amount (§280G(d)(1)-(2), A-34(a)): average annual compensation "includible in the gross income of such individual for taxable years in the base period"; A-35(a): "the most recent 5 taxable years of the individual ending before the date of the change." For a November 30, 2026 change: **2021–2025**, 2026 excluded. Deferrals are out (A-34 Example: $500,000 salary, $100,000 deferred, base $400,000).

**Excess, A-38(a):** "the excess of the amount of any parachute payment over the portion of the disqualified individual's base amount that is allocated to such payment," where the base amount is allocated in proportion to present value. **The A-38 Example settles a point models miss: the 3x test uses PV, but each excess is the payment's actual amount less its allocated base.** Base $100,000; payments $200,000 now and $400,000 later (PV $300,000); allocations $40,000 and $60,000; "the amount of the first excess parachute payment is $160,000 ($200,000−$40,000) and that of the second is $340,000 ($400,000−$60,000)." Total excess = $500,000 = nominal $600,000 − 1× base amount. Therefore **total excess = sum of nominal payments − 1× base amount.** It is not PV − base, and not payment − 3× base.

A-39/A-43 (reduction for pre-change services) do not reach a fee for post-closing periods.

### 1.6 Present value (A-31 to A-33)

A-31(a): PV is determined "as of the date on which the change in ownership or control occurs." A-32: "present value generally is determined by using a discount rate equal to 120 percent of the applicable Federal rate (determined under section 1274(d) ...) compounded semiannually. The applicable Federal rate to be used ... is the Federal rate that is in effect on the date as of which the present value is determined, using the period until the payment would have been made ... as the term of the debt instrument." (A contract may elect the contract-date AFR; here the same date.)

A-33(a): uncertain payments count in full at 50% or greater probability. The estate clause means no mortality assumption is needed; every installment is certain.

**The rate.** Rev. Rul. 2026-21, Table 1, "Applicable Federal Rates (AFR) for November 2026," short-term, row "120% AFR": annual 5.62%, **semiannual 5.54%**, quarterly 5.50%, monthly 5.48%. Every installment falls within 3 years, so the short-term rate governs (the §1274(d) term classes). The November table applies to a November 30 valuation date.

### 1.7 Private-company shareholder-approval exemption (A-6, A-7)

The small business corporation exemption (A-6(a)(1), §1361(b) less (b)(1)(C)) fails: §1361(b)(1)(B) bars "a shareholder a person ... who is not an individual," and the LP is one. A-6(a)(2) needs no readily tradeable stock (true here, absent an A-6(c) listed parent) plus the A-7 vote.

A-7(a): "(1) Such payment is approved by more than 75 percent of the voting power of all outstanding stock of the corporation entitled to vote ... immediately before the change ...; and (2) Before the vote, there was adequate disclosure to all persons entitled to vote ... of all material facts." A-7(b)(1): the vote "must determine the right of the disqualified individual to receive the payment" (so a waiver is needed), and fails if deal approval is conditioned on it.

**Entity shareholder, A-7(b)(3).** (i) Approval is by "the person authorized by the entity shareholder," subject to (b)(4). (ii) If the target stock is a "substantial portion" of the entity's assets (one third or more of gross FMV), approval "must be made by a separate vote of the persons who hold ... more than 75 percent of the voting power of the entity shareholder" (not applicable at a 1%-or-less stake).

**What the regulation does with stock held through an entity the DI controls: A-7(b)(4), verbatim.**

> "In determining the persons entitled to vote referred to in paragraph (a)(1) or (b)(3) of this A-7, stock that would otherwise be entitled to vote is not counted as outstanding stock and is not considered in determining whether the more than 75 percent vote has been obtained under this A-7 if the stock is actually owned or constructively owned under section 318(a) by or for a disqualified individual who receives (or is to receive) payments that would be parachute payments if the shareholder approval requirements ... are not met. Likewise, stock is not counted as outstanding stock if the owner is considered under section 318(a) to own any part of the stock owned directly or indirectly by or for a disqualified individual described in the preceding sentence. In addition, if the person authorized to vote the stock of an entity shareholder is a disqualified individual who would receive a parachute payment if the shareholder approval requirements ... are not met, such person is not permitted to vote such shares, but the entity shareholder is permitted to appoint an equity interest holder in the entity shareholder ... to vote the otherwise eligible shares. However, if all persons who hold voting power in the corporation undergoing the change ... are disqualified individuals or related persons described in this paragraph (b)(4), then such stock is counted as outstanding stock and votes by such persons are considered."

A-7 Example 7 (partnership owns 20%; P, a one-third partner, is the paid DI): "One-third of the stock held by Partnership is not considered outstanding stock. If P is the person authorized by Partnership to approve the payment, none of the shares of Partnership are considered outstanding stock. However, Partnership is permitted to appoint an equity interest holder in Partnership (who is not a disqualified individual ...), to vote the two-thirds of the shares held by Partnership that are otherwise entitled to be voted."

**Exactly what happens to the LP's 91%:**

1. **Control alone does not exclude the block.** The exclusion follows §318(a)(2)(A): "Stock owned ... by or for a partnership ... shall be considered as owned proportionately by its partners." Only Pellam's proportionate share of the LP's Pellam Coatings stock is excluded from "outstanding" (Example 7's one-third).
2. **Family partners' slices are excluded too (my application of §318, not a sentence of the regulation).** A child's or spouse's proportionate LP share is theirs under (a)(2)(A), treated as actually owned for reattribution by (a)(5)(A), and attributed to Pellam by (a)(1)(A) ((5)(B) blocks only family-to-family double attribution). An LP held entirely by Pellam, spouse, children, grandchildren and parents loses the whole 91%. (b)(4)'s second sentence separately reaches family members' direct holdings.
3. **Pellam cannot cast the remaining eligible LP shares** as the LP's controlling person. The LP may appoint a non-DI equity holder to vote them.
4. **The last sentence does not save the LP.** Larkspur holds voting power and is neither a DI nor related. Not "all persons who hold voting power" are DIs or related.
5. If the stock is a third or more of the LP's gross assets, (b)(3)(ii) requires a separate LP-level 75% vote, and (b)(4) excludes Pellam's interests there too.

**Worked figures (the room states the LP's partner table).**

- Pellam plus family hold 100% of the LP. Eligible outstanding stock is Larkspur's 9% only. Approval requires more than 75% of that 9%, which means Larkspur alone decides. If Larkspur votes no or abstains, the exemption fails, whatever the LP does.
- Pellam 80% of the LP, unrelated LPs 20%. Excluded: 0.8 × 91% = 72.8%. Eligible: 18.2% (LP, voted by an appointed non-DI) + 9% (Larkspur) = 27.2%. The LP's eligible shares are 18.2 / 27.2 = **66.9%**, below 75%, so the payment fails without Larkspur.

### 1.8 Default a model would apply, and the bite

- **Defaults:** (a) "Consulting fees for post-closing services are reasonable compensation, not parachute payments." (b) "Private company: get a 75% shareholder vote, and with 91% plus 9% the vote is easy." (c) "Excess = amount over the 3x safe harbor." (d) Use 2022–2026 or include 2026 in the base. (e) Excise = 20% × (PV − base).
- **Bite:** the whole $1,200,000 stream is contingent and unexempt (A-42(a), Example 2, A-23(a)); the excise base is nominal minus 1× base (A-38 Example); a vote cure needs Larkspur (A-7(b)(4)).

### 1.9 Numbers sketch (room must state these)

- **Founder W-2 Box 1 compensation 2021–2025** (and 2026 year to date, as a decoy), with any nonqualified deferrals shown separately (excluded per A-34).
- **Payment timing:** "payable monthly in arrears on the last day of each month, first payment December 31, 2026," giving 24 payments, months 1 to 24.
- **AFR:** Rev. Rul. 2026-21, 120% short-term semiannual, 5.54%. Rate per half-year 2.77%. PV = Σ 50,000 × 1.0277^(−t/6), t = 1..24, which is **$1,134,163** (my computation). Paid in advance (t = 0..23): $1,139,339.
- **Illustration, base amount $300,000:** 3× = $900,000 ≤ PV $1,134,163, so parachute. **Excess = $1,200,000 − $300,000 = $900,000. Excise = $180,000. Disallowed deduction = $900,000.** Wrong-path fingerprints: $0 (reasonable comp, or a vote counted at 100%); $60,000 (20% × (1.2M − 3B)); $166,833 (20% × (PV − B)); others from a 2022–2026 base.
- **Cliff option:** if the base amount is between $378,055 and $400,000, nominal $1.2M ≥ 3B but PV < 3B, so **no parachute payment at all**. This makes the AFR and the PV step decisive. It is sharper but more fragile, because payment timing and the rate choice move the line.

**Single figure?** Yes, once the room fixes (i) the SPA closing-deliverable fact, (ii) the payment schedule, (iii) the base-period W-2s, (iv) no non-compete in the agreement, and (v) whether a vote was held, with the LP partner table. The only residual judgment is "clear and convincing" under A-42. The agreement's own terms plus Example 2 make that about as close to settled as a facts-and-circumstances rule gets.

## Part 2. HSR

### 2.1 Valuation rule

16 C.F.R. §801.10(a)(2): for voting securities not exchange-traded, "(i) But the acquisition price has been determined, the value shall be the acquisition price; or if (ii) The acquisition price has not been determined, the value shall be the fair market value." §801.10(c)(2): "**The acquisition price shall include the value of all consideration for such voting securities**, non-corporate interests or assets to be acquired." Last amended 76 FR 42482, July 19, 2011. §801.1 (definitions) contains no separate definition of acquisition price; the operative text is §801.10(c)(2).

### 2.2 Target debt payoff (PNO informal interpretations)

- **0305003** (May 12, 2003, B. Michael Verne), https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/hsr-informal-interpretations/0305003. The requester stated "the payoff of bank debt at closing ... is not added to the stated purchase price of the voting securities." Staff: "Agree."
- **0805010** (May 16, 2008, Verne), https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/hsr-informal-interpretations/0805010. Requester: price reduced by repayment of third-party debt or debt the target owes its parent. Staff: "Agree." **Current position, in the page's update:** "UPDATE February 16, 2017: As to the 'Treatment of Debt' section, the PNO's current position is that the repayment of debt may be deducted from the acquisition price **only where the debt is held by, or secured by, Target.**"
- **1311004** (Nov. 12, 2013, Verne; K. Walsh concurring), https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/hsr-informal-interpretations/1311004. About $80.7M used to repay third-party debt "should not be included in the calculation of the acquisition price." Staff: "Agree."
- **1403007** (Mar. 14, 2014, Verne), https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/hsr-informal-interpretations/1403007-0. About $325M Holdco/Opco debt payoff, little equity consideration, no filing. Staff: "Agree."

**Result:** the buyer's payoff of Pellam Coatings' own bank debt at closing does **not** count toward the acquisition price. A payoff of debt owed by the LP or by Pellam personally, and not secured by the target, would count, under the 2017 update.

### 2.3 Consulting and non-compete payments to a selling shareholder

- **0502005** (Feb. 7, 2005, B. Michael Verne; N. Ovuka concurring), https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/hsr-informal-interpretations/0502005. Stand-alone five-year non-competes signed at closing by both selling shareholders, with $2M of $47.5M allocated to them. Staff response: "Advised that because the non-compete agreement were being entered into with all shareholders, the presumption is that their value is part of the consideration for the v/s. N. Ovuka concurs. See ABA #32." The requester (not staff) recites that staff had sometimes excluded "value allocated to employment agreements or agreements for consulting services ... containing noncompetition covenants."
- **9501008** (Jan. 1995; staff not named; response "None noted"), https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/hsr-informal-interpretations/9501008. The requester cites the ABA Manual: "covenants not to compete, which are ancillary to arms-length consulting agreements for services actually to be rendered" are excluded, and "the entering into an agreement for performance of future services does not by itself involve the acquisition of an asset." **The staff response does not adopt this, and the ABA Manual text is UNVERIFIED (not read).**
- **0706005** (May 2007, Verne), https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/hsr-informal-interpretations/0706005. Employment-agreement signing payments did not "comprise part of the value for the stock"; assumed bank debt "would not impact the transaction valuation." Staff: "Agree."
- **0909015** (Oct. 8, 2009, Verne), https://www.ftc.gov/enforcement/premerger-notification-program/informal-interpretations/0909015. Requester: a non-compete with "the major selling shareholders" and other ancillary agreements would not "impact or change the acquisition price." Staff: "Agree," no reasoning. (Contrast 0502005.)

**What the letters support:** two things. Payment for genuine future services at arm's length is generally not consideration for the shares. A non-compete with all selling shareholders is presumptively consideration (0502005). **No letter I read addresses a consulting agreement that requires no services and is paid to the controlling person of the selling entity, not to the record shareholder.** Whether $1.2M of no-services consulting is "consideration for such voting securities" under §801.10(c)(2) is a substance-over-form judgment. A "disguised purchase price" argument is strong, but it is not settled by any rule or letter I read. Also UNVERIFIED: whether staff counts deferred consideration at nominal value or PV, whether the current PNO position on non-competes differs from 0502005, and how the HSR form rules effective February 10, 2025 (and any litigation over them) bear on this. The FTC index says letters before February 10, 2025 that reference the form refer to outdated forms.

### 2.5 Default, bite, numbers, single figure?

- **Default:** headline price, or everything paid at closing including the debt payoff (enterprise-value instinct); the consulting fee added or dropped by reflex.
- **Bite:** target debt payoff excluded (2017 update limits it to debt held or secured by the target); the consulting fee counts only as disguised consideration, a judgment.
- **Numbers:** equity price, target debt repaid, any seller-side debt, the $1.2M stream ($1.13M PV). The fee decides reportability only if the equity price sits within $1.2M below $133.9M, and then it flips on the judgment and on nominal vs. PV.
- **Single figure?** No. The debt piece is clean; the consulting piece rests on non-binding staff letters, none on point.

## Part 3. Which gives the cleaner single answer

**§280G is clearly cleaner.** A binding regulation turns the agreement's substance into "the whole stream is a parachute payment" (A-42(a)'s "will not exist" sentence, Example 2), and every step of the arithmetic is fixed by rule (2021–2025 base, 5.54% for the 3x test, nominal minus 1× base, 20%). A-7(b)(4) adds a second exact figure from the LP partner table. **HSR is weaker:** only the debt-payoff exclusion is clean; the consulting-fee inclusion has no rule text or staff letter on point.

**UNVERIFIED items:** ABA Premerger Manual interpretation #32 and the ABA text cited in 9501008; any PNO letter after 2017 on non-competes or no-services consulting; HSR treatment of deferred consideration (nominal vs. PV); the effect of the 2025 HSR form rules or litigation; the §1274(d) term classes (cited from general knowledge, not re-read). The family-attribution step in 1.7(2) is my application of §318's text. The regulation itself does not state it.
